Seungmin Lee

Ph.D. student in Mathematics

My research focuses on numerical methods for partial differential equations, porous media flow, and data assimilation.

Research

Property-preserving numerical methods

Discontinuous Galerkin methods and optimization-based limiters for two-phase flow, with an emphasis on physical bounds and non-matching meshes.

I study how cell-average corrections and slope limiting can control nonphysical oscillations while retaining accuracy in heterogeneous porous media. Current work considers the challenges introduced by local mesh refinement and discontinuous material coefficients.

Porous media flow and poromechanics

Finite element discretizations and splitting algorithms for coupled flow and deformation, including Biot’s poroelasticity and two-phase flow models.

My interests include efficient coupling strategies for pressure, saturation, and displacement. I am exploring compressible two-phase poromechanics, motivated by subsurface energy storage and flow in fractured media.

Data assimilation for PDE models

Combining sparse observations with mathematical models to improve state estimates, with analysis and simulations for poroelastic systems.

My published work studies continuous data assimilation for Biot’s system. An emerging direction is the use of ensemble filtering for state and parameter estimation in two-phase flow and coupled poromechanics.

Research overview

We study continuous data assimilation for Biot’s poroelasticity system, using sparse observations to improve pressure and displacement estimates. The work combines convergence analysis with finite element simulations and a fixed-stress splitting method.
